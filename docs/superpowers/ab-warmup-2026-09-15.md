# A/B: does the warm-up barrier cut duplicate entities? (spec §9 validation)

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Status:** Complete. Graph reverted to the 999-entity baseline (999 / 655 / 655 / 0 verified).
**Runner:** `.superpowers/sdd/ab-w8-validation.py`, reverted with `ab-revert.py --yes`.

Same 83 pilot articles, same 2 sources, three arms:

| arm | wall clock | entities | exact-name dupes | after merge pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sequential (2026-09-14) | 6.92 h | 999 | 0 | — |
| N=4, **no** warm-up (2026-09-14) | 2.17 h | 1027 | **30** | 0 |
| N=4, **W=8** (this run) | 3.09 h | 1022 | **16** | **0** (1006 entities) |

83/83 articles succeeded, 655 episodes — identical to the sequential baseline.

## The variable was proven live before the spend

The 2026-09-15 first attempt spent ~$10 measuring nothing because the runner never
passed `is_cold=`. This run carried a hard smoke gate (2 articles, ~$0.25) that
refuses to continue if the predicate is never consulted.

- `cold_calls = 83` — every article consulted the predicate.
- `cold_true = 16` — and 16 is the arithmetic: 2 sources × W=8. It plateaued at 16
  and stayed there for the remaining 67 articles.
- Per-article rate during the barrier ~305 s (sequential); after it, ~101 s marginal
  (concurrent). The barrier held and then released on schedule.

**The wall clock is not the evidence, but it does corroborate rather than confound.**
Total provider in-call time was 19,007 s this run against 22,525 s for the no-warm-up
arm — the provider was 16% *faster*, yet wall clock was 43% *longer*. The slowdown
cannot be provider variance; it is the barrier.

The cost model closes to 0.1%: 16 cold × 305 s + 67 warm × 93 s = 11,111 s predicted
against 11,123 s measured.

## The warm-up worked — and the residual is a different bug

Duplicates fell 30 → 16 (47%), short of the predicted ~6. Spec §9 says to treat a
residual far above 6 as the risk model being wrong for this fan-out shape. It is
wrong, but not in magnitude — **in population**.

|  | no warm-up (30 dupes) | W=8 (16 dupes) |
|---|---|---|
| mean span of duplicated entities | 7.6 | **2.0** (max 2) |
| corpus mean span | 2.4 | 4.34 (max 358) |

The no-warm-up duplicates were **hub entities** — entities spanning many articles,
where any two of the many landing concurrently before the first commit creates a
twin. Warm-up eliminated that class **entirely**: not one residual duplicate has
span > 2.

Every one of the 16 residuals comes from just **two adjacent article pairs**:

| dispatch positions | source ordinals | entities duplicated |
|---|---|---|
| 32 / 33 | 14 / 15 | 2 — `Active resources`, `Deprovisioned resources` |
| 71 / 72 | 47 / 48 | 13 — Azure region names from one availability table |

These are sibling documents from the same doc family (`backup-azure-immutable-vault-concept`
and `-how-to-manage`; `tutorial-review-security-posture` and `tutorial-view-protected-items`),
sharing an entity set that appears nowhere else in the corpus.

## Why the risk model missed it

`scripts/risk_curve.py` models collisions as probabilistic draws weighted by entity
span, assuming article ordering is independent of article content. Both assumptions
fail here:

1. **The articles are adjacent.** At concurrency 4, consecutive articles are
   *guaranteed* in flight together. P(collision) = 1, not p.
2. **The entities are correlated.** 13 of them enter the graph in the same table, in
   both articles, for the first time. Span-2 entities got near-zero weight in the
   model precisely because they are rare individually — but they arrive in a
   correlated batch.

**Warm-up cannot fix this class, at any W.** These entities do not exist in the graph
before either article starts, so there is nothing for a warm article to resolve
against. Catching the pos-71/72 pair would have required W = 48 — effectively
sequential ingest, which is what the branch exists to avoid.

## The merge pass is the right answer for the residual

16 groups planned, 16 merged, 0 remaining. Edges rewired: 6 `RELATES_TO`, 17
`MENTIONS`, 0 self-loops created. Pure Cypher, no LLM, seconds.

Final entity count 1006 against the sequential 999 (+7). The A/B established that
~28% of extracted names differ run-to-run, so a single-digit delta is extraction
nondeterminism, not residual duplication.

## Recommendation: keep W=8

The 42% wall-clock penalty measured here is **an artefact of the pilot's shape, not
the corpus's**. Warm-up cost is fixed per source:

| | pilot | full corpus |
|---|---|---|
| sources | 2 | 280 |
| articles | 83 | 126,405 |
| sequential warm-up articles (W=8) | 16 = **19%** | 2,240 = **1.8%** |

The relative cost falls by a factor of ten at full scale, while the benefit — removing
the hub-entity duplicate class, which grows with corpus size as hub spans grow — persists.

So: **warm-up for the hub class, merge pass for the sibling-pair class.** They are
complementary, addressing populations neither can reach alone. Both arms reach 0
duplicates after the merge pass; warm-up's value is doing less damage in the first
place, which matters because the merge pass rewires edges and the fewer it must touch,
the smaller the blast radius.

## Open question for the user

The sibling-pair class is deterministic and therefore predictable. A cheap mitigation
exists that was not in the spec: **shuffle the dispatch order** so that sibling
articles are not adjacent. That converts a guaranteed collision into a ~1-in-20 one at
c=4 without costing any throughput. It is not free of risk — it interacts with the
`already_ingested` replay gate and with resumability — and it has not been evaluated.
Worth a look before the full bootstrap.
